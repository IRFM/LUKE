
The starting point of the flux conservative representation is the
conservation of the total number of particles in the plasma, 
\begin{equation}
N=\idotsint f\left( \mathbf{X},P\right) d^{3}\mathbf{X}d^{3}\mathbf{P}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{P}$ are respectively coordinates in
configuration and momentum spaces. According to the systems which are used
in the calculations, $\mathbf{X=}\left( \psi ,\theta ,\phi \right) $ and $%
\mathbf{P=}\left( p,\xi ,\varphi \right) ,$%
\begin{equation}
\left\{ 
\begin{array}{l}
d^{3}\mathbf{X=}\frac{Rr}{\left| \nabla \psi \right| \left| \widehat{\psi }%
\cdot \widehat{r}\right| }d\psi d\theta d\phi  \\ 
d^{3}\mathbf{P=}p^{2}dpd\xi d\varphi 
\end{array}
\right. 
\end{equation}
as shown in Appendix ****, on obtains 
\begin{equation}
N=\idotsint f\left( \psi ,\theta ,\phi ,p,\xi ,\varphi \right) \frac{Rr}{
\left| \nabla \psi \right| \left| \widehat{\psi }\cdot \widehat{r}\right| }%
p^{2}d\psi d\theta d\phi dpd\xi d\varphi 
\end{equation}

Using the transformation 
\begin{equation}
\frac{1-\xi ^{2}}{B\left( \psi ,\theta \right) }=\frac{1-\xi _{0}^{2}}{
B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }
\end{equation}
that results from conservation of the magnetic moment and the energy, 
\begin{equation}
N=\idotsint f\left( \psi ,\theta ,\phi ,p,\xi ,\varphi \right) \frac{Rr}{
\left| \nabla \psi \right| \left| \widehat{\psi }\cdot \widehat{r}\right| }
p^{2}\frac{\xi _{0}}{\xi }\frac{B\left( \psi ,\theta \right) }{B_{\min
}\left( \psi \right) }d\psi d\theta d\phi dpd\xi _{0}d\varphi
\end{equation}
since $\frac{\xi d\xi }{B\left( \psi ,\theta \right) }=\frac{\xi _{0}d\xi
_{0}}{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }.$ Since at the order, $f_{0}$ is
constant along a magnetic field line, it is independent of the poloidal
angle $\theta ,$ and $f_{0}=f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }$ where $f_{0}^{\left(
0\right) }$ is the bounce averaged distribution function. Hence 
\begin{eqnarray}
N &=&\iiint f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right)
p^{2}d\psi dpd\xi _{0}\int_{\theta _{\min }}^{\theta _{\max }}\frac{Rr}{%
\left| \nabla \psi \right| \left| \widehat{\psi }\cdot \widehat{r}\right| }%
\frac{\xi _{0}}{\xi }\frac{B\left( \psi ,\theta \right) }{B_{\min }\left(
\psi \right) }d\theta \int_{0}^{2\pi }d\phi \int_{0}^{2\pi }d\varphi 
\nonumber \\
&=&4\pi ^{2}\iiint f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right) 
\frac{1}{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }p^{2}d\psi dpd\xi _{0}\int_{\theta
_{\min }}^{\theta _{\max }}\frac{Rr}{\left| \nabla \psi \right| \left| 
\widehat{\psi }\cdot \widehat{r}\right| }\frac{\xi _{0}}{\xi }B\left( \psi
,\theta \right) d\theta
\end{eqnarray}
taking into account, in addition, of the toroidal aximmetry, and cylindrical
symmetry of the distribution along the magnetic field line direction. Here, $
\theta _{\min }$ and $\theta _{\max }$ depends of the particle trajectory in
the configuration space, wether they are passing or trapped.

From plasma equilibrium, since 
\begin{equation}
\frac{\left| \nabla \psi \right| }{R}=B_{P}
\end{equation}
where $B_{P}$ is the poloidal magnetic field, 
\begin{equation}
\int_{\theta _{\min }}^{\theta _{\max }}\frac{Rr}{\left| \nabla \psi \right|
\left| \widehat{\psi }\cdot \widehat{r}\right| }\frac{\xi _{0}}{\xi }B\left(
\psi ,\theta \right) d\theta =\int_{\theta _{\min }}^{\theta _{\max }}\frac{r%
}{\left| \widehat{\psi }\cdot \widehat{r}\right| }\frac{\xi _{0}}{\xi }\frac{
B}{B_{P}}d\theta =2\pi \lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) \widetilde{q}
\left( \psi \right)
\end{equation}

Here, appears, as expected the normalized bounce time $\lambda \left( \psi
,\xi _{0}\right) $ and the factor $\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) $ which
in conjunction with $B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) $ characterizes the local
shape of magnetic flux surface. Hence, 
\begin{equation}
N=8\pi ^{3}\iiint f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right) 
\frac{\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }%
\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) p^{2}d\psi dpd\xi _{0}
\end{equation}

From this expression, the Jacobian $J$ of the coordinate system $\left( \psi
,p,\xi _{0}\right) $ may be simply defined as, 
\begin{equation}
J=\frac{\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }%
\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) p^{2}
\end{equation}
and the generic conservative form of the kinetic equation may be immediatly
deduced 
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }}{\partial t}+\nabla _{\left( \psi
,p,\xi _{0}\right) }\cdot \mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }\left( f_{0}^{\left(
0\right) }\right) =0
\end{equation}
where the phase space flux $\mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }$ at $B=B_{\min }$
is decomposed into a diffusive and a convective part 
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }\left( f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) =-\Bbb{D}%
^{\left( 0\right) }\nabla f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }+\mathbf{F}^{\left(
0\right) }f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }
\end{equation}
$\Bbb{D}^{\left( 0\right) }$ and $\mathbf{F}^{\left( 0\right) }$ being
respectively the diffusion tensor and convection vector in phase space. They
can be expressed generally as 
\begin{equation}
\Bbb{D}^{\left( 0\right) }=\left( 
\begin{array}{lll}
D_{pp}^{\left( 0\right) } & D_{p\xi }^{\left( 0\right) } & D_{p\psi
}^{\left( 0\right) } \\ 
D_{\xi p}^{\left( 0\right) } & D_{\xi \xi }^{\left( 0\right) } & D_{\xi \psi
}^{\left( 0\right) } \\ 
D_{\psi p}^{\left( 0\right) } & D_{\psi \xi }^{\left( 0\right) } & D_{\psi
\psi }^{\left( 0\right) }
\end{array}
\right) 
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}^{\left( 0\right) }=\left( 
\begin{array}{l}
F_{p}^{\left( 0\right) } \\ 
F_{\xi }^{\left( 0\right) } \\ 
F_{\psi }^{\left( 0\right) }
\end{array}
\right) 
\end{equation}
where each element is function of $\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right) $. So,
following calculations given in Appendix ***, 
\begin{eqnarray}
\nabla _{\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right) }\cdot \mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right)
}\left( f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\right)  &=&\frac{1}{J}\frac{\partial }{%
\partial p}\left( J\mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }\cdot \mathbf{e}^{p}\right) +%
\frac{1}{J}\frac{\partial }{\partial \xi _{0}}\left( J\mathbf{S}^{\left(
0\right) }\cdot \mathbf{e}^{\xi }\right) +\frac{1}{J}\frac{\partial }{%
\partial \psi }\left( J\mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }\cdot \mathbf{e}^{\psi
}\right)   \nonumber \\
&=&\frac{1}{J}\frac{\partial }{\partial p}\left( J\mathbf{S}^{\left(
0\right) }\cdot \widehat{p}\right) -\frac{1}{J}\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial }{%
\partial \xi _{0}}\left( \sqrt{1-\xi _{0}^{2}}J\mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }%
\cdot \widehat{\xi }\right) +\frac{1}{J}\frac{\partial }{\partial \psi }%
\left( J\left| \nabla \psi \right| _{\min }\mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }%
\cdot \widehat{\psi }\right)   \nonumber \\
&=&\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial }{\partial p}\left( p^{2}\mathbf{S}%
_{p}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) -\frac{1}{\lambda }\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial 
}{\partial \xi _{0}}\left( \sqrt{1-\xi _{0}^{2}}\lambda \mathbf{S}_{\xi
}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) +\frac{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }{\lambda 
\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }\frac{\partial }{\partial \psi }\left( 
\frac{\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }%
\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) \left| \nabla \psi \right| _{\min }%
\mathbf{S}_{\psi }^{\left( 0\right) }\right) 
\end{eqnarray}
where $\left| \nabla \psi \right| _{\min }$ is taken at $B=B_{\min }.$ The
first two terms correspond to the usual dynamics in momentum space at a
given spatial position $\psi $, while the third one is associated to spatial
transport at fixed $p$ and $\xi _{0}$. It is interesting to note that
spatial transport is not independent of the momentum dynamics through the
parameter $\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) .$ It corrects the spatial
transport from the particle dynamics along the magnetic field line, since
most particles tend to spend more time far from $B=B_{\min }$. In the limit
of strongly passing particles, $\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) $ $%
\simeq 1$, and the spatial term becomes independent of $\xi _{0}.$

It is interesting to cross-check the conservative nature of the transport
equation is well ensured by performing the integral 
\begin{eqnarray}
\iiint \left[ \frac{\partial f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }}{\partial t}+\nabla
_{\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right) }\cdot \mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }\left(
f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) \right] Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi &=&0  \nonumber \\
\frac{\partial N}{\partial t}+\iiint \nabla _{\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right)
}\cdot \mathbf{S}^{\left( 0\right) }\left( f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\right)
Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi &=&0
\end{eqnarray}

Indeed, in that case, the variation of the total number of particles $\frac{%
\partial N}{\partial t}$ as a function of time depends only from boundary
conditions. 
\begin{equation}
\iiint \nabla _{\left( \psi ,p,\xi _{0}\right) }\cdot \mathbf{S}^{\left(
0\right) }\left( f_{0}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi =\mathcal{%
I}_{p}-\mathcal{I}_{\xi _{0}}+\mathcal{I}_{\psi }
\end{equation}
where 
\begin{equation}
\left\{ 
\begin{array}{l}
\mathcal{I}_{p}=\iiint \frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial }{\partial p}\left(
p^{2}\mathbf{S}_{p}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi \\ 
\mathcal{I}_{\xi _{0}}=\iiint \frac{1}{\lambda }\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial }{%
\partial \xi _{0}}\left( \sqrt{1-\xi _{0}^{2}}\lambda \mathbf{S}_{\xi
}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi \\ 
\mathcal{I}_{\psi }=\iiint \frac{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }{\lambda 
\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }\frac{\partial }{\partial \psi }\left( 
\frac{\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }%
\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) \left| \nabla \psi \right| \mathbf{S}%
_{\psi }^{\left( 0\right) }\right) Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi
\end{array}
\right.
\end{equation}

For $\mathcal{I}_{p},$ 
\begin{eqnarray}
\iiint \frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial }{\partial p}\left( p^{2}\mathbf{S}%
_{p}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi &=&\iint \left[ p^{2}%
\mathbf{S}_{p}^{\left( 0\right) }\right] _{0}^{p_{\max }}\frac{\widetilde{q}%
\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi
_{0}\right) d\xi _{0}d\psi  \nonumber \\
&=&p_{\max }^{2}\iint \mathbf{S}_{p}^{\left( 0\right) }\left( p_{\max
}\right) \frac{\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi
\right) }\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) d\xi _{0}d\psi
\end{eqnarray}
and assuming that $\lim_{p\rightarrow \infty }p^{2}\mathbf{S}_{p}^{\left(
0\right) }=0,$ one finds $\mathcal{I}_{p}=0.$ This condition is generally
well fullfiled, except in strong runaway regimes, where above the Dreicer
limit characterized by critical momentum $p_{D},$ electrons gain energy up
to very high energies, that are usually well beyond the domain of
integration addressed in numerical calculations for the current drive
problem. However, in this case, $\mathcal{I}_{p}$ is given by $\mathbf{S}%
_{p}^{\left( 0\right) }$ at $p_{\max }$, where $p_{\max }$ corresponds to
the boundary of the momentum domain of integration. Its conservative nature
is well, since $\frac{\partial N}{\partial t}$ only depends of this
parameter for $p$.

The integration of $\xi _{0}$ leads to 
\begin{eqnarray}
\mathcal{I}_{\xi _{0}} &=&\iiint \frac{1}{\lambda }\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial 
}{\partial \xi _{0}}\left( \sqrt{1-\xi _{0}^{2}}\lambda \mathbf{S}_{\xi
}^{\left( 0\right) }\right) Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi =\iint \left[ \sqrt{1-\xi
_{0}^{2}}\lambda \mathbf{S}_{\xi }^{\left( 0\right) }\right] _{-1}^{+1}\frac{%
\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }pdpd\psi 
\nonumber \\
&=&0
\end{eqnarray}
which indicates that pitch-angle scattering never contributes to variations
of $N$, and therefore the conservative nature of this term in the transport
equation is well demonstrated.

Finally, 
\begin{eqnarray}
\mathcal{I}_{\psi } &=&\iiint \frac{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }{\lambda 
\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }\frac{\partial }{\partial \psi }\left( 
\frac{\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }%
\lambda \left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) \left| \nabla \psi \right| \mathbf{S}%
_{\psi }^{\left( 0\right) }\right) Jdpd\xi _{0}d\psi =\iint \left[ \frac{%
\widetilde{q}\left( \psi \right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi \right) }\lambda
\left( \psi ,\xi _{0}\right) \left| \nabla \psi \right| \mathbf{S}_{\psi
}^{\left( 0\right) }\right] _{0}^{\psi _{a}}p^{2}dpd\xi _{0}  \nonumber \\
&=&\frac{\widetilde{q}\left( \psi _{a}\right) }{B_{\min }\left( \psi
_{a}\right) }\left| \nabla \psi \right| _{\psi _{a}}\iint \lambda \left(
\psi _{a},\xi _{0}\right) \mathbf{S}_{\psi }^{\left( 0\right) }\left( \psi
_{a}\right) p^{2}dpd\xi _{0}
\end{eqnarray}
which only depends of edge values at $\psi _{a}.$ Again, if $\mathbf{S}%
_{\psi }^{\left( 0\right) }\left( \psi _{a}\right) =0,$ $\mathcal{I}_{\psi
}=0,$ and the total number of particles is conserved in the discharge.

It is important to notice that
